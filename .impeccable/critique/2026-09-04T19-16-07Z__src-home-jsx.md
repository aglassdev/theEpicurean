## Overall Impression

This is the strongest thing in the project and it looks the part — a genuine luxury food-atlas voice, executed with discipline. But the two most conspicuous elements are the two least finished: the richest-looking section (Dispatches) is entirely non-functional, and the signature interaction (the carousel) excludes keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, and touch-swipe users. The single biggest opportunity: make the page *behave* as finished as it *looks* — wire the dead links and make the carousel operable by everyone — before any further visual polish.

## What's Working

- **A fully committed editorial world.** The palette, Cormorant/EB Garamond/Jost stack, kickers, ※ divider, and section marks cohere into one voice. Nothing reads as a template; this is the project's biggest asset.
- **The overture-to-carousel sequence.** A calm text hero (single-ink headline, italic lede, hairline gold rule) resolving into the 78vh full-bleed cover image creates real magazine cadence and anticipation.
- **Credibility architecture with no fabrication.** The animated figures table (12,650 / 3,397 / 163) and the grayscale journals marquee do the persuasive heavy lifting — scale + authority — while honoring the "no fake evidence" product principle.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The Dispatches section is dead.** All three article cards *and* "All Journals →" resolve to `href="#"`. It's the most editorial, most clickable block on a Persuade page, and every click dead-ends silently — a trust puncture mid-scroll that violates the "no half-finished surfaces ship" principle.
- *Fix:* wire the cards to their article routes and "All Journals →" to `/news` (the News page already exists). If the article destinations aren't ready, show them as unlinked editorial rather than clickable cards.
- *Suggested:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The carousel is inaccessible and un-pausable.** Slides and indicators are `onClick` `<div>`s with no `tabindex`/`role`/`aria-label`; cover images are CSS backgrounds with no alt (screen readers get only 38× `alt="Star"`); it auto-advances every 5s with no pause and no `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback (also affects the 55s marquee and the count-up).
- *Fix:* make slides real links, make indicators tabbable controls labeled with slide names, add a pause/stop affordance, and gate motion behind reduced-motion.
- *Suggested:* `/impeccable harden`

**[P2] Small-label contrast fails AA.** Gold `#A8824A` on paper = **3.29:1** and inkMute `#8B8377` on paper = **3.50:1** — both below 4.5:1 for the 10–11px uppercase text where they live (every kicker and meta label). Large gold accents pass at 3:1 and are fine.
- *Fix:* reserve gold for large text; darken the label-gold or switch kickers/meta to `inkSoft` (6.95:1).
- *Suggested:* `/impeccable colorize`

**[P2] Mobile sticky nav eats the viewport.** At 375px the masthead stacks into three rows for a **128px** sticky bar — ~16% of the screen pinned on every scroll.
- *Fix:* collapse to a single ~56px row (wordmark + condensed/menu control) on small screens.
- *Suggested:* `/impeccable adapt`

**[P3] Value proposition is diffuse.** The hero overture has no entry action, and the actual differentiator ("the one place that reconciles all the journals into one atlas") is never stated as a benefit — while "a hundred sources" is repeated three times (hero lede, colophon, footer). A first-timer isn't told why this beats checking each guide.
- *Fix:* lead one line with the unification benefit, add a quiet hero entry action, and trim the triple "hundred sources" refrain.
- *Suggested:* `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan (first-timer):** clicks the gorgeous Dispatches cards and nothing happens — the first real interaction is a dead-end; never told the core value, so may not grasp why to trust or return over a Google search.
- **Riley (stress-tester):** can't operate the carousel by keyboard at all; a screen reader announces "Star Star Star" and no restaurant names; "All Journals" and every dispatch click fail silently; every nav item triggers a full-page reload (`window.location.href`) instead of client routing; gold kickers wash out on paper.
- **Casey (mobile):** meets a 128px stacked sticky nav; the 78vh carousel isn't swipeable and its only controls are 2px-tall ticks (far below a 44px target); the entire slide is one tap target that routes to a restaurant page, so an exploratory tap becomes an accidental navigation.
- **Vivian (project persona — affluent food traveller, choosing a special-occasion table):** gets the mood instantly but no path to *act* on it — no "find a table near me / in a city," no way to save a place; the featured ten are inspiring but she can't tell how to get from "beautiful" to "book the anniversary dinner."

## Minor Observations

- `slide.textColor` (`#333`/`#fff`) is defined per carousel item but never read — the overlay is always `paper`; on bright covers it leans only on the `.62` bottom scrim, so legibility is image-dependent. Dead data + a latent contrast risk.
- Numbering is internally inconsistent: hero "No. 01", carousel "Plate I", then "Section II–V" — a reader can't place the hero/carousel in the section count. (The *device* is intentional and correct; only the counter coherence is the note.)
- "The Featured" appears three times in the top fold (hero kicker, "Plate I · The Featured", per-slide "No. 0X · The Featured").
- The cloned last carousel slide duplicates slide 0's route/alt (functionally fine).
- Footer byline "a glass production" and the single "Sections" column are tasteful and on-brand.

## Questions to Consider

- The most beautiful section on the page is the only fully broken one. Does an obviously-dead editorial block do more damage to the "built to be proud of" bar than simply omitting it until News is wired?
- For a surface whose differentiator is *unification*, should the hero say it outright ("Michelin, La Liste, the World's 50 Best — reconciled into one atlas") instead of leaving the marquee to imply it 1,300px down?
- The signature interaction is exactly the element that excludes keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, and touch users. Is an auto-carousel the right vehicle for the featured tables — or would a static editorial grid of covers persuade *and* include more of the audience?
