Last week, chef Bruno Verjus announced that this December, La Table in Paris will be closing, which he claims is by his personal choice only. He took this announcement as a platform to declare to the world that "fine dining is dead." A chef as awarded as him, with two Michelin Stars and two consecutive Top 10 finishes in The50Best surely knows what he is talking about...right? If you take about two seconds to look behind the thin layer of bullshit in his words, you can see the much bleaker truth about his current standing, and more importantly, about his blatant hypocrisy.

First, let’s start with his restaurant’s closure. It’s really not a huge surprise. Restaurant turnover has been extreme for years, they are notoriously fine-margin establishments operating in a cut-throat industry. Even in a holy site such as Paris, we have seen many fine dining restaurants shut their doors due to unprofitability. But Verjus’s lack of revenue didn’t come from higher food prices, or rent, or wages. No, it actually came from his own ego. His restaurant offered one tasting menu, priced at €480 per person, which is at the high end, even in fine dining, especially considering all nine three-star establishments in the city offer cheaper menus. As people realized that there are better value-for-money restaurants in the city, fewer and fewer seats got filled at La Table, and Bruno couldn’t afford to set it anymore.
